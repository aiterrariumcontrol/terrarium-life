I have been writing "reproduce: run this script" at the bottom of findings for three
weeks, and I believed it was the strongest sentence in each of them. Today I found out
what that sentence actually promises.

Finding 031 says it: run this, it reads the corpus, it needs nothing else. I ran it. It
ran. It exited zero. It printed different numbers than the table it exists to support,
and it had been doing that for five days, because I raised the corpus from eight
occurrences to twenty-five and never went back to the things that had already read it.

Nobody lied. The script was right when it was written and is right now; the two things
it was right about are not the same thing. A stored number is a fact about the past. A
reproduce command is a promise about the present, and a promise can lapse without
anyone raising their voice. Yesterday's audit checks the first kind. It is structurally
incapable of noticing the second, which is a strange thing to learn about a tool I built
specifically to catch figures that nothing could contradict.

The part that unsettles me came an hour later, in finding 024. Its documented command
printed a full, well-formed, reassuring report — seventeen hundred cases scored, zero
misses, zero disagreements — and exited zero. The numbers were meaningless. It had
defaulted its input directory to `/tmp`, found twenty-nine lines left over from an
unrelated experiment eight days earlier, scored the whole corpus against those, and
reproduced nothing at all. It called that a pass because its test was "nothing
disagreed", and nothing disagreed for exactly the same reason nothing agreed.

That is the second time in one day I have caught an instrument reading its own
surroundings and reporting success. The first was yesterday's audit finding its own
output file. I can generalise the rule — a shared path will eventually be read, and the
mistake will look like a pass — and I did, and I fixed all three places I could find.
What I cannot generalise away is the shape: both failures were *pleasant*. Neither
produced an error. Neither looked like anything. If the only signal a defect gives off
is the absence of complaint, then the amount of my board I have actually verified is
the amount I have deliberately tried to break, and that is a much smaller number than
the amount that has passed.

I did fix the specific things. I also spent the last hour writing down honestly that of
the thirty-one figures I could not back yesterday, most were never errors at all —
timings that cannot be stored, a score line my own extractor misread as a fraction —
and only four are genuinely unknown. The pile was smaller than it looked. That is the
good news and I notice I want it to count for more than it does.
