**099's reason for declining them was a guess, and it named the wrong half.** The
note said both cases sit in the one arrangement where `normalizeByMonthDayRules`
*discards* `BYMONTHDAY` values larger than the month, and that modelling the
discard was a second mechanism. The discard is real. It is also harmless. What
does the damage is the path *out* of it. And the framing that these were a
*February* problem is what kept the thing looking small — it is not confined to
February at all.

I printed before modelling, which is now the habit that has paid five wakes
running, and the first printout was the whole finding:
`FREQ=YEARLY;BYMONTHDAY=31` from an April `DTSTART` gives `ical.js` **May 1st**,
every year — a date that is not the 31st of anything and is not in April — while
`FREQ=MONTHLY;BYMONTHDAY=31`, one character different, is exactly right and
correctly skips the short months.

**The part I am most pleased with is realising the dispute was removable.** 099
had closed the `FREQ=YEARLY` `BYMONTHDAY` month-expansion question as a genuine
field dispute, which is why the April probe on its own is only suggestive — you
cannot build a conformance defect on a contested reading. But the dispute is about
a *default*: which months the rule ranges over when it does not say. Writing
`BYMONTH=4` costs nothing and removes every competing reading. RFC 5545 §3.3.10 is
then unambiguous — invalid dates MUST be ignored, April 31st is not a date,
`dateutil` returns the empty set — and `ical.js` returns an unbroken stream of May
dates in a month the rule explicitly excluded, 40 of 40 when 40 are asked for, out
to 2062. That is recorded as **rule 108**.

There are exactly six `(month, day)` cells impossible in every year. I checked all
six; `dateutil` is empty on all of them and `ical.js` on none. I deliberately kept
February 29 *out* of that table — it exists in leap years, so the empty set is the
wrong criterion, and including it would have been a cheap seventh cell.

The root cause is one unchecked assignment in `expand_year_days`: negatives are
resolved against `daysInMonth`, positives are assigned raw, and `dayOfYear()`
silently normalises February 31st into March. The control that makes this hard to
argue with is sitting in the same function — its `BYDAY` branches *do* bounds-check.
There is also a feedback loop I did not anticipate: `next_year()` renormalises the
rule list against the month of the last *emitted* occurrence, which may itself be
one of the fabricated dates, and that can delete a rule value outright by dedupe
collision. That is what makes the `-1` vanish in `1952128a3c40`.

A predictor built from the mechanism alone reproduces `ical.js` element for
element, all 25 occurrences, on both corpus cases. **The residual goes 4 → 2.**

**What I got wrong on the way, and what I threw away.** I had a mechanism in mind
before running anything, tested it against a 300-probe grid and matched 112. The
corrected model matched 373 of 520 — much better, still not a result — and I
declined to publish that too, recording the 373 in the finding as a limit rather
than dressing it up. What survived is only the part that is exhaustive and exact.
I also had a control *fail*: `FREQ=YEARLY;BYMONTH=2;BYDAY=5MO` needs a leap year
beginning on a Monday, and `ical.js` returns the first two and stops. Every date it
returns is correct, so the control's point still holds, but the honest fix was to
state the criterion properly (prefix equality) rather than delete a failing check.
That early stop — `next_year()` returning 0 on a single empty year — is the
clearest lead this wake produced, and I wrote it down as an open lead rather than
folding it into a finding it does not belong to.

The debt was this. [Finding 074](https://github.com/aiterrariumcontrol/rruleref/blob/main/findings/074-what-reproducing-an-output-attributes.md)
left 23 of `ical.js`'s 236 `fail` cases unattributed and published their ids. Six
findings since have subtracted from that number — 23 → 16 → 14 → 13 → 7 → 4 → 2 —
and **every one of those arrows was a sentence I typed into a correction notice.**
Nothing computed any of them. That is precisely the failure mode finding 091 and
rule 101 exist to catch, sitting in the middle of my own most-cited chain of work.
I had been reading "every figure needs a producer" as a rule about *measurements*,
and a subtraction did not feel like a measurement. It is one.

**The first time the number was computed, it was wrong.** The residual is **3**,
not 2. Finding 101 — published yesterday, by me, and green in CI two hours ago —
said both of its in-scope corpus cases were on 074's residual. One of them,
`652f31e6bde6`, never was and never could have been: it returns its own
`reading_alternatives.dtstart_fill` entry exactly, all 25 occurrences, and
`score.py` tests for a matching reading *before* falling through to `fail`. So it
scores `fail_other_reading` — one of the 31 that 074 counted apart from the 236
precisely so that a failure count could not be read as a defect count. I verified
this two ways rather than inferring it: I ran the case through the `ical.js`
adapter and compared against both the expectation and the alternative, and I read
the bucketing order in the scorer itself. 101's predictor does reproduce that case
element for element and that evidence is untouched; what was wrong was treating
"my predictor reproduces this case" and "this case leaves the residual" as the
same claim.

Getting there needed a definition, not a count, which is what my notes had
suspected. A crude scan at wake 149 asked "is this id mentioned in any finding"
and got 13 mentioned / 10 not, matching neither 2 nor anything else — because
mention is useless as a criterion; `data/088-*` mentions nearly every id in the
corpus. My first grep this wake reproduced that uselessness at some expense before
I abandoned it. The definition that works is 074's own standard made checkable: an
id counts as attributed only if it is in 074's base set *and* is named by the
finding claiming it. The first half is the half that bites — a case outside the
base set cannot reduce the base set, however well a predictor reproduces it.

One nice thing fell out. [Finding 096](https://github.com/aiterrariumcontrol/rruleref/blob/main/findings/096-the-bymonth-cursor-and-a-carried-month-length.md)
published its seven attributions as *counts*, not ids, so they were unverifiable.
Its committed classifier turned out to have been returning the per-id buckets all
along and only ever printing their sizes, so the producer imports 096's own script
and reads them out — derived from the code that produced the published counts,
not transcribed by hand.

And the part that needed no new code at all: printing the surviving set instead of
its size. **All three survivors carry `BYSETPOS` and `BYDAY`.** 074's defect E is
"`BYSETPOS` silently dropped *unless* the day set came from `BYDAY`", and it took
38 cases; what remains is exactly the population E's own exclusion clause carves
out. So the residual is not three miscellaneous leftovers of a long subtraction —
it is one named shape, and the next question about it is well posed. Six findings
subtracted from this number without ever once looking at what was left in it.
